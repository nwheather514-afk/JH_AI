3. Accounts

  • Use AI for work only through company accounts created with a company email. Never put work material into personal accounts.
  • Do not share one AI account or password among several people.
  • Turn on multi-factor authentication for email and AI tool accounts.
  • When someone leaves, their accounts are removed on their last day.

4. What must never go into AI

  • Files that contain a customer's name together with an ID number (SSN, etc.)
  • Bank account and card numbers
  • Original tax returns and original financial records [add items for your industry]
  • Passwords and authentication codes
  • Original contracts and confidential material
  • [Any other items your company prohibits]

If it is truly necessary, get [owner's name]'s approval first and use only a copy with names and numbers removed or replaced. Example: "John Smith" becomes "Client A," and a number becomes "[number]."

5. Checking AI output

  • Verify any numbers, legal explanations, and dates from AI against the source documents.
  • Anything sent to a customer is reviewed by the responsible person first.
  • Check [industry rules and contracts] to see whether customers must be told that AI was used.

6. If a mistake or incident happens

  1. If you enter sensitive information by mistake or suspect misuse, tell [owner's name / contact] right away.
  2. Delete the conversation and record what was entered.
  3. [Owner] decides whether customers or authorities need to be notified.

Employees who report a problem themselves will not be penalized.

7. Reviews and training

  • Each month, in [the first week], review the AI tools and accounts in use.
  • Read this policy together and train at least once a year. New hires do it in their first week.
